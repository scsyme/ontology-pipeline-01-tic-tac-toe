# Comparing candidate terms for one board location

**Proposal for review.** Group the conventional-game usages of square, space and cell around a provisional meaning, one of the nine locations in the 3×3 board that can hold a player's mark. Hold position for further interpretation. Scott's subsequent selection of cell is recorded below. The subsequently agreed definition is recorded in [T003](../terms/T003.md).

## Referent and substitution

The following are our interpretation tests.

- S001 calls the nine locations spaces and continues to use space when one is filled.
- S004 calls the nine mark-bearing locations squares. Substituting cells or spaces preserves that description.
- S005 uses squares for the numbered destinations and space for an occupied destination. Substituting either noun in the other instruction preserves its meaning.
- S006 counts cells and then qualifies a cell as vacant. Substituting square or space in these placements preserves the described action.
- S002's variant usages also refer to individual locations. They support a related lexical usage, with their board and rule differences recorded.
- S005's position may denote a destination, but its API context also permits an encoded return value. The evidence is insufficient to approve it as an interchangeable label.

The S006 adjective describing the grid illustrates why matching spelling alone cannot determine equivalence.

## Occupancy and permission to play

Our interpretation is that a board location persists when filled. S001 and S005 supply explicit occupied-location wording. Vacancy qualifies the location in S006.

The stopping-rule reviews in S005 and S006 establish that a location can remain empty after play ends. Defining the proposed shared meaning as an available move would exclude such a location.

## Decisions for review

1. Accept or revise the proposed grouping of the conventional-game occurrences of square, space and cell.
2. Decide whether S002's variant occurrences are useful supplementary evidence within their recorded limits.
3. Resolve the meaning of position before considering its inclusion.
