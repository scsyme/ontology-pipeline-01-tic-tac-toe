# Do square, space and cell refer to the same thing?

**Proposal for review.** Group these three candidate usages around one provisional meaning, a single location in the conventional 3×3 board that can hold a player's mark. Confidence is high for the recorded passages. Preferred wording and a formal definition remain open.

## Evidence and interpretation

The linked term records hold the excerpts and passage locators. The following comparisons record our interpretation of those occurrences.

| Candidate | What the passage refers to | What it tells us about occupancy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [T001, square](../terms/T001.md) | One of the nine numbered board locations described in S005, page 1, second paragraph. | The passage counts the board's locations without qualifying them by occupancy. |
| [T002, space](../terms/T002.md) | A board location the player is forbidden to use because it is already filled, in the next sentence of S005. | A filled location is still called a space. |
| [T003, cell](../terms/T003.md) | A location in the grid where a player places a mark, in S006, page 1, opening paragraph. | The adjective vacant specifies the location's current condition. |

S005 uses square and space in successive instructions about the same board. S006 supplies the matching grid context for cell. This supports grouping the three usages. The proposal depends on their context and referents.

## Substitution check

These are our paraphrases, used to test meaning.

- In the numbered-input instruction, replacing squares with cells or spaces still identifies the board's nine locations.
- In the occupied-location restriction, replacing space with square or cell preserves the restriction.
- In the placement instruction, replacing vacant cell with vacant square or vacant space preserves the requirement that the chosen location is empty.

The substitutions preserve the relevant instructions in these passages. They support a shared meaning, while later evidence could reveal additional senses that need their own treatment.

## Occupancy and permission to play

Our interpretation is that the location persists when its occupancy changes. The occupied-space occurrence directly supports that distinction. Empty, occupied and legally available describe different conditions relevant to the same location.

The [S005](../sources/S005.md) and [S006](../sources/S006.md) stopping-rule reviews show why this matters. An empty location can remain after a win, when play has ended. Defining the shared meaning as an available move would therefore exclude a location that still exists on the board.

## Limits and review decision

This comparison covers three occurrences from two sources. [S002, Wolfram MathWorld](../sources/S002.md), remains in the candidate corpus and awaits passage-level assessment for this comparison. It provides a local basis for grouping their meanings. General usage of square, space and cell extends beyond this experiment. A future occurrence referring to the whole square board, for example, would require a separate reading.

The decision for review is whether to accept the proposed grouping for these occurrences. All three term records have candidate status. Selection of a preferred label and approval of a definition are subsequent decisions.
